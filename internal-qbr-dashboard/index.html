Competitive risk low today

Our read: low right now, and for operational reasons rather than contractual ones. Nothing in the account names a competitor — all 20 columns and all three notes were searched. So this is a judgement from what the deployment does, not from market intelligence we do not have.

Why it is low

  • Replacing us means 3,394 tickets a day going back to Northstar's people, on top of the 1,450 they already handle. Their Tier-1 human load has already fallen 5.8% since launch — that is capacity they would need to find again.
  • A replacement starts from zero on the two things that make this work. The assistant is tuned on Northstar's own support history and answers from a live index of their product catalogue. Neither transfers to another vendor.
  • A competitor has to beat a rising baseline. All 14 measures improved over the month, so whatever anyone else demos has to beat where we will be, not where we started.

Why it is not lower

  • Nothing contractual holds the account. No term, renewal date or service commitment exists anywhere in the materials. What keeps Northstar is that the deployment works, which is a choice they get to remake at any time.
  • One month is not a moat. Switching costs are real but young, and they are lowest before a second brand is added.

What would raise it: the quality gap widening, or reaching a renewal conversation still with no service levels agreed. Both already have owners and dates on this dashboard.

What we cannot see: who else Northstar evaluated, what they pay, when they renew, or what the market is offering. Those need the account team, not the data.

Inference cost is fully measurable and is rising faster than volume. Everything else — staffing, infrastructure, revenue, margin — is absent, so margin cannot be computed and is not estimated here.

What inference actually costs, and where it is going

Cost per resolved ticket rose 25.3% in 31 days while the price of a model call did not move. Tokens per request are flat at 1,381 → 1,373. The deployment is taking more calls to resolve each ticket — 4.62 at launch, 6.21 now, up 34.3%.

The two cost figures we can calculate from the file

Both come straight from the file. Nothing is estimated.

1   Inference spend, as a monthly run rate

Take what the deployment cost over the last seven days of data and scale it to a 30-day month.

Spend, 25–31 Aug  =  $322.42
÷ 7 days  =  $46.06 per day
× 30 days  =  $1,382 per month

For reference, the whole 31 days cost $1,213.12. The run rate is higher than the month's average because usage grew across the month.

2   Cost per ticket the system resolved

Divide that same week's spend by the number of tickets it closed without a person.

Spend, 25–31 Aug  =  $322.42
÷ tickets resolved without a person  =  23,757
=  $0.0136 per resolved ticket

Worth saying out loud: that is about one and a third cents to resolve a support ticket. Even if it doubled it would still be a rounding error next to the cost of a person handling the same ticket.
